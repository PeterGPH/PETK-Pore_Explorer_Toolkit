Second, against the sharp-walled analytical reference, the \emph{uncorrected} map conductance lies $8$--$20\%$ low, with the deviation largest at small diameter---the signature of a fixed inward wall displacement. Mapping the ramp onto its electrically equivalent sharp wall removes this deficit almost entirely, but a quantitative comparison also has to correct the reference itself. Equation~\eqref{eq:kowalczyk} uses Hall's access resistance $\rho/2d$, which is exact for an aperture in a thin plate and $4.6\%$ too small for any membrane thicker than about a pore radius, where the exact end correction is that of a flanged tube (Appendix~\ref{app:sigma}). Evaluated at the area-conserving offset $(d-0.54~\text{nm},\,L+0.54~\text{nm})$, Equation~\eqref{eq:kowalczyk} therefore drifts from the converged map conductance by $0.0\%$ at $d = 5$~nm to $+2.0\%$ at $d = 25$~nm ($L = 20$~nm), and by up to $3.1\%$ across $d = 5$--$50$~nm and $L = 1$--$40$~nm. This drift is the share of the access term growing with $d$, not a failure of the soft-wall correction. With the exact finite-thickness access term and the equivalent sharp geometry of Appendix~\ref{app:sigma}---the equal-area pore diameter, the membrane faces displaced by the same deficit length, and a small rounded-rim correction---the closed form reproduces the converged map conductance to $0.03\%$ (maximum) over $d = 2$--$50$~nm and $L = 1$--$40$~nm, with no parameter fitted to the solid-state pores being benchmarked [Figure~\ref{fig:si_softwall}]. In the finite golden-aspect box of Section~\ref{sec:domain} the map conductance sits $-0.24\%$ to $+0.54\%$ from its infinite-reservoir value, so the residual against the reference in Figure~\ref{fig:analytical_validation}(A) is box truncation, not geometry. \PENDING{regenerate Figure~\ref{fig:analytical_validation}(A) against Equation~\eqref{eq:semfull}; the $\pm 2.5\%$ band becomes a $\pm 0.5\%$ band set by the box.}

\section{Mapping the conductivity map onto a sharp-walled pore}\label{app:sigma}
This appendix derives the sharp-walled geometry that is electrically equivalent to the ramped conductivity map of a cylindrical solid-state pore, and the sharp-walled reference against which it is compared. Every statement is checked against an axisymmetric finite-element solution of $\nabla\!\cdot\!(\sigma\nabla\phi) = 0$ with unbounded reservoirs, converged to $\sim\!10^{-5}$ in conductance (\texttt{sem.axisym\_conductance}).

\paragraph{Conductivity ramp.}
The conductivity is $\sigma(\mathbf{r}) = \sigma_0\,f(D(\mathbf{r}))$, where $D$ is the Euclidean distance to the membrane and $f$ ramps linearly from $0$ at $r_\mathrm{min}$ to $1$ at $r_\mathrm{cut}$:
\begin{equation}
f(D) =
\begin{cases}
0, & D \le r_\mathrm{min},\\[2pt]
\dfrac{D - r_\mathrm{min}}{r_\mathrm{cut} - r_\mathrm{min}}, & r_\mathrm{min} < D < r_\mathrm{cut},\\[8pt]
1, & D \ge r_\mathrm{cut},
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{aligned}
r_\mathrm{min} &= 1.3~\text{\AA},\\
r_\mathrm{cut} &= 4.1~\text{\AA}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:framp}
\end{equation}
On a flat wall the ramp removes the conductance of a layer of thickness
\begin{equation}
t = \int_0^\infty \bigl[1 - f(D)\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}D = \tfrac{1}{2}(r_\mathrm{min}+r_\mathrm{cut}) = 2.7~\text{\AA},
\label{eq:deficit_t}
\end{equation}
which is the natural candidate for the wall displacement. The question is where, and how exactly, it applies. The resistance of the pore has three parts---the bore, the membrane faces and access region, and the rim where they meet---and the ramp enters each differently.

\paragraph{Bore: equal-area radius, exact.}
Inside the bore $\sigma$ depends only on $r$, and $\phi = -Ez$ satisfies both $\nabla\!\cdot\!(\sigma\nabla\phi) = 0$ and the insulating condition on the wall. The field is therefore exactly axial, the conducting layers add in parallel, and the channel conductance per unit length is $\sigma_0\int_0^a f\,2\pi R\,\mathrm{d}R = \sigma_0\pi a_e^{2}$ with
\begin{equation}
a_e^{2} = a^{2} - (r_\mathrm{cut}+r_\mathrm{min})\,a + r_\mathrm{cut}^{2} - \Delta r\,r_\mathrm{min} - \tfrac{2}{3}\Delta r^{2},\qquad \Delta r = r_\mathrm{cut} - r_\mathrm{min},
\label{eq:matching}
\end{equation}
valid for $a \ge r_\mathrm{cut}$. No normal (``series'') contribution enters, because no current crosses the iso-$\sigma$ surfaces. For the default ramp $a_e = \sqrt{(a - 2.7~\text{\AA})^{2} + 0.654~\text{\AA}^{2}}$; the curvature term moves $a - a_e$ from $2.700$ to $2.685$~\AA\ at $d = 5$~nm, a $0.1\%$ effect in conductance.

\paragraph{Faces and access region: the same offset.}
Outside the pore the current converges radially onto the mouth, and along the membrane faces it again runs tangentially, parallel to the ramp. To leading order the face ramp is therefore equivalent to moving each face outward by $t$. For a sharp-cornered membrane that displacement is exactly a change of thickness, $L \to L + 2t$, by translation invariance, so the access region needs no separate treatment: the radial (``$\mathrm{d}r$'') convergence of the access current is already accounted for by the same deficit length.

\paragraph{Rim: a rounded corner.}
Because $D$ is a Euclidean distance, the iso-$\sigma$ contours wrap the rim as quarter circles, so the equivalent wall is the Minkowski offset of the membrane, whose rim is rounded with radius $\approx t$, rather than a sharp-cornered offset. The sharp rim is a $270^{\circ}$ fluid corner whose field is singular as $\varrho^{-1/3}$ (distance $\varrho$ from the edge). Rounding it over a length $t$ shortens each end of the equivalent pore by
\begin{equation}
\Delta\ell = c(\lambda)\,t^{4/3}a_e^{-1/3},\qquad \lambda = (L + 2t)/a_e,
\label{eq:rim}
\end{equation}
the $t^{4/3}a_e^{-1/3}$ scaling following from the corner singularity. Extracted from the converged solutions, $c$ collapses onto a single curve of $\lambda$ for every $d$ from $2$ to $50$~nm [Figure~\ref{fig:si_softwall}(D)]: $c = 0.53$ for $L \gtrsim a$, rising to $0.64$ at $\lambda = 0.06$, where the plate edge begins to look like a knife edge. (Here $\Delta\ell$ is defined through the thickness $L_e$ of the sharp-walled pore of radius $a_e$ that conducts like the map, $\Delta\ell = (L + 2t - L_e)/2$, so that the access coefficient below is evaluated consistently at $L_e/a_e$.) $\Delta\ell$ is $0.71$~\AA\ per end at $d = 5$~nm and $0.40$~\AA\ at $d = 25$~nm, so the thickness offset is $2(t - \Delta\ell) = 0.40$--$0.46$~nm rather than $0.54$~nm. A sharp wall placed on the $D = t$ iso-surface recovers $65$--$85\%$ of this for $d \ge 5$~nm; the rest comes from the ramp's weighting around the curved rim and the bore. $c$ depends on the ramp shape only through $\Delta r/t$ (from $0.73$ for $r_\mathrm{min} = 0$ to $0.46$ for a step), and with that dependence included the closed form below holds to $0.015\%$ for $d \ge 3$~nm for ramps with $r_\mathrm{cut} = 3$--$6$~\AA\ and $r_\mathrm{min} = 0$--$2.5$~\AA; the shape dependence is calibrated on a single thick-membrane geometry per ramp, so none of these $(d, L)$ solutions entered a fit.

\paragraph{The sharp-walled reference: Hall's access term is a thin-membrane limit.}
Writing the resistance of a sharp-walled cylinder of radius $a$ as
\begin{equation}
R_\mathrm{sharp} = \frac{\rho}{\pi a^{2}}\bigl[L + 2\,\kappa(L/a)\,a\bigr],
\label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:kowalczyk} corresponds to $\kappa = \pi/4 = 0.7854$, the end correction of an equipotential disc. This is exact for an aperture in a thin plate ($L \to 0$), where the mouth is equipotential by symmetry. In a membrane of finite thickness it is not, and $\kappa$ rises to the flanged-tube value $\kappa_\infty = 0.82155 \pm 0.00002$ (cf.\ $0.8216$ in the acoustics literature \PENDING{cite Norris \& Sheng 1989 after checking}) already at $L \approx a$; it is within $5\times10^{-4}$ of $\kappa_\infty$ for $L \ge a$. Hall's access term is therefore $4.6\%$ too small for practically every solid-state pore, and Equation~\eqref{eq:kowalczyk} overestimates the conductance by up to $3.2\%$ (at $L \approx 0.2\,d$), $2.3\%$ at $L = 0.8\,d$, and $0.8\%$ at $L = 4d$. The approach to $\kappa_\infty$ is exponential, with the decay rate $j_{1,1} = 3.8317$ of the first non-trivial mode of an insulating cylinder; a closed form that is exact at both limits and within $6\times10^{-5}$ in between is
\begin{equation}
\kappa(\lambda) = \kappa_\infty - \bigl(\kappa_\infty - \tfrac{\pi}{4}\bigr)\,e^{-j_{1,1}\lambda}\bigl[w + (1-w)\,e^{-c_\kappa\lambda^{2/3}}\bigr],\qquad w = 0.496,\; c_\kappa = 4.19.
\end{equation}
For $L \ge d/2$ it suffices to replace $1/d$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:kowalczyk} by $1.046/d$.

\paragraph{Combined mapping.}
The open-pore resistance of the conductivity map of a cylinder of nominal diameter $d = 2a$ and thickness $L$ is then
\begin{equation}
R_\mathrm{SEM} = \frac{\rho}{\pi a_e^{2}}\bigl[L_e + 2\,\kappa(L_e/a_e)\,a_e\bigr],\qquad L_e = L + 2t - 2\Delta\ell,
\label{eq:semfull}
\end{equation}
with $a_e$ from Equation~\eqref{eq:matching} and $\Delta\ell$ from Equation~\eqref{eq:rim}. Against the converged map conductance it is accurate to $0.03\%$ (maximum; $0.007\%$ r.m.s.) over $d = 2$--$50$~nm and $L = 1$--$40$~nm, and to $0.18\%$ at $d = 1.5$~nm, outside the fit. Each ingredient matters [Figure~\ref{fig:si_softwall}(B,C)]: Hall's access term at the area-conserving offset errs by up to $3.1\%$ (and $-10.6\%$ at $d = 1.5$~nm, $L = 1$~nm), and the exact access term without the rim correction by up to $-7.4\%$ for thin membranes and small pores. With Equation~\eqref{eq:semfull} the offset needed for agreement no longer drifts with $d$. The diameter offset stays at $2(a - a_e) = 0.537$--$0.540$~nm, set by the ramp alone. The drift from $0.52$ to $0.95$~nm reported against Equation~\eqref{eq:kowalczyk} is reproduced by the continuum solution and is entirely the access-term error.

\paragraph{Reconciliation and robustness.}
The same area-conservation relation links the parametric map to an all-atom membrane: inverting Equation~\eqref{eq:matching} slice by slice, $a(z) = \tfrac{1}{2}(r_\mathrm{min}+r_\mathrm{cut}) + \sqrt{r_\mathrm{eq}(z)^{2} - 0.654~\text{\AA}^{2}}$ with $r_\mathrm{eq}(z)=\sqrt{A_\mathrm{eff}(z)/\pi}$ measured on the all-atom map, gives the parametric radius that reproduces the all-atom open cross-section; for the drilled membranes of Table~\ref{tab:open_pore_current_match} it lies $\approx 0.9$~\AA\ inside the atom-centre drill radius. That comparison is between two soft walls built from the same ramp, so no offset enters; the analytical comparison needs the full mapping because Equation~\eqref{eq:kappa} assumes a sharp wall. At the reference point $d = 10$~nm, $L = 20$~nm, $\sigma = 10.5$~S/m, Equation~\eqref{eq:semfull} gives $26.18$~nS against $29.61$~nS for the nominal sharp pore and $26.39$~nS for Equation~\eqref{eq:kowalczyk} at the area-conserving offset; the golden-aspect three-dimensional value, $26.3$~nS, lies $\approx 0.5\%$ above the first, consistent with the box truncation measured above. The inverse map---the nominal SEM geometry that conducts like a given sharp-walled pore, for instance an experimental one---follows from Equation~\eqref{eq:semfull} by fixed-point iteration (\texttt{sem.open\_pore\_theory.sem\_cylinder\_for\_sharp\_pore}).
